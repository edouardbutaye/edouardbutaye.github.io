Research and CFD solver development for turbulent and multiphase flows

My work sits between the physics of turbulent and multiphase flows and the codes used to simulate them. I characterise these flows, derive and assess the models that describe them, and implement those models in production CFD solvers. It covers the whole chain, from a physical question to the C++ implementation, its validation against experimental data, and the non-regression test cases that keep it working for the other users of the code.

Now Postdoctoral researcher at CEA Saclay, implementing the hybrid RANS-LES method HTLES in TrioCFD and applying it to the turbulent flow around nuclear fuel assemblies, with A. Burlot.

Most of my development work is carried out in TRUST/Trio_CFD, the open-source platform developed by CEA, where the methods I implement are made available to the other users of the code. During my PhD I added to it the computation of the hydrodynamic forces and of the heat flux on resolved particles, an optimised collision detection that reduced the overall computation time by 20%, and an improved treatment of the phase indicator at the faces and edges of the control volumes. I have also worked with NEPTUNE_CFD, Code_Saturne and SYRTHES, and the simulations are run in high performance computing, on up to 3 800 processors on national supercomputers, including GPU porting with Kokkos.

The physics behind these developments concerns the scale at which a flow has to be resolved. Resolving every scale is rarely affordable in an industrial configuration, and the models used at coarser resolutions are often calibrated on academic cases far removed from the ones in which they are applied. My work consists in quantifying what a given resolution leaves unresolved, and in building corrections and criteria that make that choice explicit rather than left to the judgement of the person setting up the computation.

I completed my PhD in 2024 at the PROMES laboratory of the University of Perpignan Via Domitia, on particle-resolved simulation of anisothermal fluid-particle flows for concentrated solar power, under the supervision of A. Toutant, S. Mer and F. Bataille.

Research

  • Hybrid RANS-LES simulation

    Implementation of the HTLES method in TrioCFD, and definition of a physical criterion to locate the regions of the domain that require an unsteady description.

  • Particle-resolved simulation

    Hydrodynamics and heat transfer in anisothermal fluidized beds, and a subgrid-corrected resolution scale for simulations that cannot afford to resolve the boundary layers.

  • Two-phase boiling flows

    Euler-Euler simulation of boiling flows in the receiver tubes of direct steam generation plants, with conjugate heat transfer in the tube wall.
